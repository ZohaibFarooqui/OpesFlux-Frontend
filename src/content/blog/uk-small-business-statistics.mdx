## How many businesses are there in the UK?

There were **5.6 million private sector businesses** in the UK at the start of 2026, according to DBT's business population estimates. That is 104,000 fewer (−1.8%) than in 2025.

The fall is entirely among one-person and owner-only businesses. Businesses with employees **rose by 54,000 (3.8%)**, while businesses with no employees **fell by 157,000 (3.7%)**.

Over a longer period, business numbers rose from 4.5 million in 2010 to a peak of 6.0 million at the start of 2020. The 2026 total is 394,000 (6.6%) below that peak.

### Businesses by size, start of 2026

| Size (employees) | Businesses | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No employees | 4,115,175 | 73.7% |
| Micro (1 to 9) | 1,198,870 | 21.5% |
| Small (10 to 49) | 225,215 | 4.0% |
| Medium (50 to 249) | 39,220 | 0.7% |
| Large (250 or more) | 8,355 | 0.15% |
| **Total** | **5,586,835** | **100%** |

The micro, no-employee and total figures are from DBT's Table C. We worked out the small, medium and large rows from DBT's totals for businesses with 0 to 49 employees (5,539,260) and SMEs (5,578,480). Shares are rounded.

In other words, about 1.5 million UK businesses employ anyone at all, and fewer than 50,000 employ more than 50 people.

### Employment and turnover

- Private sector businesses employed **28.3 million people** at the start of 2026, up from 28.1 million.
- SMEs employed **17 million** of them, **60%** of private sector employment.
- SMEs had an estimated turnover of **£2.78 trillion**, **50%** of the private sector total of £5.54 trillion. DBT's turnover figures exclude the financial and insurance sector.

### By sector and region

- Construction has the most SMEs: **846,000**, or 15% of all SMEs.
- Wholesale and retail trade has **523,000** SMEs (9%).
- London had **1.01 million** private sector businesses and the South East **864,000**. Together they hold about a third (33.5%) of the UK business population.

Source: [DBT, Business population estimates for the UK and regions 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2026/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2026-statistical-release), published 5 November 2026.

## How many businesses are VAT registered?

HMRC counted **2,330,400 active VAT-registered traders** in the 2024 to 2025 tax year, 11,400 more than the year before. During the year there were **234,000 new registrations** and **218,000 deregistrations**, leaving **2,285,900 traders** live at the end of the year.

- **Wholesale and retail is the largest sector** in the VAT population, with 511,300 traders (22% of the total). It is also the largest contributor to net home VAT, at £57 billion (32%).
- **38% of VAT-registered traders** declared annual turnover at or below the VAT registration threshold. That means they registered voluntarily, often so they can reclaim VAT on purchases or because their customers are VAT-registered businesses.
- Traders with turnover over £10 million paid **75%** of net home VAT.
- Total VAT receipts for 2024 to 2025 were **£171 billion**: £162 billion of home VAT and £9 billion of import VAT.

Source: [HMRC, Annual UK VAT statistics 2024 to 2025 commentary](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/value-added-tax-vat-annual-statistics/annual-uk-vat-statistics-2023-to-2024-commentary).

### The VAT threshold

You must register for VAT if your taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over **£90,000**, or if you expect it to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. Source: [GOV.UK, Register for VAT](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat).

### Registered businesses on the ONS business register

A different count, from the ONS Inter-Departmental Business Register, found **2.79 million businesses registered for VAT and/or PAYE** in March 2026, up 1.9% on March 2025. The largest industry group is professional, scientific and technical activities. Source: [ONS, UK business: activity, size and location 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/bulletins/ukbusinessactivitysizeandlocation/2026).

**Why the counts differ:** DBT's 5.6 million includes an estimate of unregistered businesses (mainly sole traders below the VAT threshold with no employees). HMRC's figure counts VAT-registered traders only, and the ONS register counts businesses registered for VAT, PAYE or both. Use the figure that matches your question.

## How many businesses start and close each year?

From the ONS annual business demography release for 2024:

| Measure | 2023 | 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business births | 316,000 | 317,000 |
| Birth rate | 11.0% | 11.1% |
| Business deaths | 310,000 | 280,000 |
| Death rate | 10.8% | 9.8% |

- The 2024 death rate of 9.8% was the **lowest since 2016**.
- There were about **2.9 million active businesses** in 2024 on this measure.
- **Transport and storage** had both the highest birth rate (15.6%) and the highest death rate (16.6%).
- **London** had the highest business birth rate, at 12.7%.
- There were **14,330 high-growth businesses** in 2024.

### How many businesses survive five years?

Of businesses born in 2019, **38.4%** were still trading in 2024. Put the other way, about six in ten new businesses close within five years.

Source: [ONS, Business demography, UK: 2024](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/bulletins/businessdemography/2024), published 20 November 2025.

## Late payment

The government estimates that late payment:

- costs the UK economy **almost £11 billion a year**,
- **closes 38 UK businesses every day**, and
- affects **over 1.5 million businesses**.

Source: [DBT, Late payments consultation: tackling poor payment practices](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/late-payments-tackling-poor-payment-practices/late-payments-consultation-tackling-poor-payment-practices), issued 31 July 2025.

## What the numbers mean if you run a small business

- **Most UK businesses are very small.** Three in four have no employees, and only about 1.5 million employ anyone. Tools built for large companies rarely fit.
- **Hiring is growing even as total numbers fall.** The number of employing businesses grew by 3.8% in a year, so more small firms are taking on payroll for the first time.
- **Wholesale and retail is the biggest VAT sector**, with more than half a million registered traders, and stock, margins and VAT are a large share of the paperwork.
- **Cash flow decides survival.** With fewer than four in ten new businesses lasting five years, and late payment closing dozens of firms a day, sending invoices promptly and chasing them matters.
